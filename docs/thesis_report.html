Time-LLM+: Multimodal LLM Forecasting for Vietnamese Stocks

Thesis progress report · branch thesis/multimodal · data through 28 Oct 2026

Summary
  • C1, event-grounded news prompting: holds up. Real pre-open news in the prompt beats the original Time-LLM prompt, our earlier data-derived prompt and a shuffled-news control (pooled AUC 0.541 vs 0.489 / 0.490 / 0.508).
  • C2, news-conditioned spectral filter bank: partially holds up. It is the best patching variant (0.550) and beats our earlier FFT+attention patching (0.528). Period-aligned patching does not help, and post-event time scales barely differ by event type.
  • C3, regime handling: no learned mechanism is robust, because market relationships flipped sign around 2023. Walk-forward retraining of a regime-neutral model is the robust fix: 57.3% winrate, +1.23% per trade out of sample (2025–26).
  • The 60% winrate target was reached on the development years (61.9% / 62.3%), not out of sample.
  • Against 15 state-of-the-art methods: Time-LLM+ is the best model trained on our data (pooled AUC 0.517, vs 0.455 for the original Time-LLM and at most 0.441 for PatchTST, iTransformer, TimeMixer, TimeXer, CALF, TaTS, MM-TSFlib and REST-TS). Zero-shot foundation models are stronger still: TimesFM 3 (0.557, 58.7% win) and Chronos-2 (0.543).

1. What is Time-LLM?

Time-LLM (Jin et al., ICLR 2024) repurposes a frozen large language model for time-series forecasting. Instead of fine-tuning the LLM, it translates the series into a form the LLM can read:

Input series→Patching (fixed length/stride)→ Reprogramming: cross-attention to word-embedding prototypes→ Prompt-as-Prefix + soft tokens→Frozen LLM→Output projection (MSE forecast)
  • Patching: the series is cut into fixed-length overlapping patches, each embedded as a token.
  • Reprogramming: patch embeddings attend to a small set of text prototypes (learned linear combinations of the LLM's word embeddings), so the time series is expressed in the LLM's own embedding space.
  • Prompt-as-Prefix: a text prefix describes the dataset and task and gives input statistics (min, max, median, trend, top lags).
  • Frozen backbone: only patching, reprogramming and the output head are trained, which keeps the trainable parameter count small.

2. Weaknesses of Time-LLM (for stock prediction)

WeaknessWhy it matters for stocksEvidence in this project
The prompt adds no new informationThe prefix only restates statistics of the same series the model already sees. The LLM's language ability is never used for information the prices lack.Price-only models of every kind (LSTM, DLinear, PatchTST, gradient boosting) show no next-day signal (IC ≈ 0; direction accuracy ≈ the always-down floor).
Fixed patchingOne patch length for all inputs, chosen by hand, regardless of what is happening to the stock.Our earlier FFT+attention patching only re-weighted fixed patches (the "weighted sum" critique).
Plain MSE objectiveRegression on noisy returns ignores direction, trade selection and market regime.Our earlier hybrid loss scored well pooled (AUC 0.559) but collapsed in 2024 (0.467).
No handling of regime shiftA model fitted on bull years is used unchanged in a falling market.Relationships flipped sign around 2023 (§8).
Evaluation pitfalls (in our earlier pipeline)Inflated results.Features were forecast one channel at a time, the test set was checked every epoch, and prices were not adjusted for corporate actions. All fixed.

Protocol

  • No look-ahead in news: an item counts for day t only if it was published before the 09:00 open of t; date-only timestamps are treated as end of day.
  • Purged rolling-origin splits: test years 2023, 2024, 2025–26; training and validation label windows never overlap the next split.
  • Floors: every winrate is shown next to the trivial "take every event in the majority direction" rule for that year. In rising years this alone wins ≈58%.
  • Controls: shuffled-news inputs; bootstrap and permutation significance tests.
  • Development vs holdout: 2023–24 for design decisions; 2025–26 as the check. 2025–26 has been viewed several times during the project, so it is supporting evidence, not a pristine holdout.

5. Our method: Time-LLM+

Time-LLM+ keeps Time-LLM's frozen backbone (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct; 7B tested) and reprogramming, and changes three components. About 23M parameters are trainable; the LLM is frozen.

ComponentOriginal Time-LLMTime-LLM+
C1 PromptDataset/task description + statistics of the input seriesEvent-grounded news prompt + knowledge tokens
C2 PatchingFixed patch length and strideKnowledge-conditioned adaptive spectral decomposition (KASD)
C3 Objective / regimeMSE forecast, fitted onceRegime handling (several mechanisms tested; walk-forward adaptation retained)

C1 · Event-grounded news prompting

  • The prompt carries the company's pre-open news (up to 8 items, last 5 trading days), each with the frozen LLM's structured reading: event type, sentiment and age.
  • The same structured information is also given as 8 learned knowledge tokens (event, sentiment, age and source embeddings).
  • Unlike our earlier "dynamic prompt", which was generated from the same price indicators, this adds information the time series does not contain. That answers the critique that the prompt was "just data".
Input series: <ts> × 60 News knowledge: <kn> × 8 Pre-open news about the company: - [ownership, sentiment -1, today] MBB: SIC chưa mua 1.000.000 cp - [management, sentiment +1, 2 sessions ago] MB bổ nhiệm ... vào Ban điều hành Will MBB rise above today's opening price after 30 trading days?

C2 · Knowledge-conditioned adaptive spectral decomposition (KASD)

  • Learnable band-pass filter bank: 5 Gaussian filters in the frequency domain, initialised at periods of 48 / 20 / 10 / 5 / 2.5 days, with learnable centres and widths. The input is split into band components by FFT → filter → inverse FFT.
  • News hypernetwork: reads the knowledge summary, the market state and news statistics, plus a per-event-type spectral prior, and shifts each band's centre and width per sample. The text changes which components are extracted, not just how they are weighted.
  • Response-spectrum supervision (training only): the bands are fitted, as a mixture over frequency, to the spectrum of the realised post-event return path. This gives the conditioning a direct learning signal.
  • Period-aligned patching (optional): each band is patched with a patch length equal to its own period, via differentiable resampling.
  • A diversity penalty keeps the bands apart. Band tokens are reprogrammed into the LLM space as in Time-LLM.

C3 · Regime handling

Four learned mechanisms were developed and tested (§8): a fixed-sign anchor, selective trading heads (profit-aware, evidence-aware, regime-conditioned), a regime-neutral selective head, and regime-adaptive re-centring with a learned drift gate. None was robust. The retained C3 is walk-forward adaptation:

  • a regime-neutral regression objective (MSE on the 30-day return, no fixed-direction prior);
  • quarterly retraining with a purged 12-month validation window;
  • a symmetric trade rule: long the top 10% of scores and short the bottom 10%, with thresholds set on validation.

Together with the analysis of why the learned mechanisms fail, this forms an empirical contribution.

How each method was run

  • Numeric: Time-Series-Library reference code (commit 4e938a1) with TSLib's default hyperparameters; TimeMixer uses its own script settings.
  • Zero-shot foundation models: official packages (Chronos-2, TimesFM 3, TimesFM 2.5), 256 days of log-price history plus past covariates, no training on our data.
  • TaTS (ICLR 2026, commit a053503) and MM-TSFlib (NeurIPS 2024, commit e789ce7): their text-fusion mechanisms with repository defaults, fed the same Vietnamese news embeddings (BGE-M3). TaTS's Time-MMD-specific "prior" column does not exist for this data and is not used.
  • REST-TS (arXiv 2606.19413) has no public code; it is re-implemented from the paper.
  • CALF (commit 50e7270) uses its own model and loss in an environment with its pinned library versions.
  • Time-LLM: re-implemented in our framework (statistics prompt, fixed patches, MSE).
  • Patches to cloned code, all behaviour-preserving and recorded in stock_llm/sota/*_local_patch.diff: TSLib's in-place normalisation made out-of-place; CALF's missing get_peft_model import added.

What the comparison shows

  • Trained forecasters are anti-predictive Every model trained on our data, apart from Time-LLM+, scores a pooled AUC of 0.38–0.46: PatchTST, iTransformer, TimeMixer, TimeXer, CALF and the original Time-LLM. They learn the 2020–22 momentum and extrapolate it into the 2023–26 reversal regime (§8).
  • Published text fusion does not fix this TaTS, MM-TSFlib and REST-TS stay within ±0.03 of their numeric backbones (0.39–0.44). This agrees with the 2026 "text collapse" and "Semantics or Structure?" findings: the text branch adds little once the numeric backbone dominates.
  • Time-LLM+ is the best model trained on our data Pooled AUC 0.517, versus 0.455 for the original Time-LLM and at most 0.441 for every trained competitor. It is the only trained model above 0.5 in two of three years.
  • Zero-shot foundation models are stronger TimesFM 3 (0.557, 58.7% win, +1.67% per trade) and Chronos-2 (0.543) beat everything trained here, mainly through 2025–26 (0.58–0.59). Pre-training on large, diverse corpora avoids fitting this market's regime. A natural next step is to use a foundation-model forecast as the numeric backbone or anchor inside Time-LLM+, combining its robustness with the news signal from C1.

8. C3: why learned regime mechanisms fail

Every C3 mechanism tested

#MechanismWhat it learns from the pastOutcome
1Fixed-sign anchor (sentiment + 60-day reversal)ReversalTraining years showed momentum, so it learned nothing
2Decomposition + Group DRO + ranking + selective head + conformal thresholdWorst-regime robustnessHurt AUC; conformal threshold never certified any trades
3–6Selective heads: plain, hybrid, profit-aware, regime-conditionedWhen to tradePassed against MSE, failed against fair references
7Evidence-aware selective headWhen to trust the news signal73% / 70% in development, 46.9% out of sample; 100% longs
8Regime-neutral selective head (long bias removed)Selection on both sidesNo better than simple score-based selection
9Regime-adaptive re-centring with a learned drift gateWhether market drift persistsAUC 0.255 in 2023 (systematically backwards)
–Walk-forward MSE (retained)Nothing fixed; re-estimated each quarter57.3%, +1.23% per trade out of sample

The common cause: relationships flipped sign around 2023

Relationship2020–22 (training)2023–242025–26
Recent market drift → next 30-day return (correlation)+0.18 (persists)−0.28 (reverses)−0.16 (reverses)
Share of events followed by a 30-day rise≈57%57–59%48%
News-sentiment weight fitted on training yearsbelow 0.1 (hit the floor)sentiment alone predictive out of sample (AUC 0.545 / 0.570 in 2023 / 2024)

Any mechanism that learns a fixed mapping from market state or news to direction inherits the training period's regime. Once that regime ends, it is confidently wrong. The selective head's 70%+ development winrate was long bias meeting a rising market. Frequent re-estimation is the only approach that survived. This explains why static models, including the original Time-LLM, fail in this market.

9. Limitations and next steps

  • One seed for most comparisons; AUC differences of about 0.01 are within noise. Only large gaps should be relied on (C1 news vs non-news; the drops from decomposition and DRO).
  • The 2025–26 period has been viewed many times. The cleanest confirmation is a forward test: freeze the final model and evaluate it on November 2026 onward.
  • Small universe: 8 stocks and ≈7,000 events. Extending to the full VN30 would tighten all estimates and allow a fresh holdout.
  • Turning points: quarterly retraining lags by one quarter (2025 Q4). Monthly retraining is a cheap next experiment.
  • Backbone: 7B was not reliably better than 1.5B and is about 4× slower.
